The charge

Build nothing you cannot scan, release, and explain.

Graduates leave with a platform they can defend: infrastructure in code, a pipeline that stops when a scan fails, a Kubernetes release, and the signals that say whether it is healthy.

01

Practice

Seventy percent of the hours are labs, projects, and review. Three sessions a week. Two to four hours between them.

02

Judgement

A working demo is not enough. Cost, failure, access, and the scan result are graded with the feature.

03

Proof

The capstone is a system another engineer could operate from the runbook, without a private explanation.

For beginners with sound computer skills, students, developers, and engineers moving into this work. Basic Linux is useful. A short orientation is provided before Week 1.

The syllabus

Foundations

01

Linux and the shell

The filesystem, users, permissions, and sudo as controls. Packages, processes, systemd, logs, and Bash.

EvidenceA least-privilege baseline and a scripted health report.

02

Networking and SSH

Addressing, DNS, TLS, firewalls, keys, patches, and a host baseline of ports and failed logins.

EvidenceKey-based access between two hosts, and a troubleshooting runbook.

03

Git, GitLab, and secret scanning

Branches, merge requests, protected branches, and a scanner that stops a credential before it merges.

EvidenceA reviewed project, and a planted secret that does not land.

04

AWS fundamentals

IAM, MFA, EC2, S3, VPC, security groups, budgets, tagging, and CloudTrail.

EvidenceA locked-down web server and an S3 site, with the security and cost written down.

Provision

05

Docker, and the image scan

Multi-stage builds, Compose, volumes, health checks, and a scan whose critical findings are defects.

EvidenceAn application and database, and a scan report with the serious findings fixed.

06

AWS architecture

Auto Scaling, a load balancer, Lambda, CloudWatch, private subnets, and secrets management.

EvidenceA highly available sample, and a Lambda with an alarm.

07

Terraform, scanned before apply

Workflow, state, modules, environments, and an infrastructure scan of the plan.

EvidenceA modular network and compute stack, applied only after the scan, then destroyed.

08

Ansible

Roles, Vault, idempotence, and a hardening pass. Terraform builds the machine. Ansible configures it.

EvidenceNginx from a role. The second run changes nothing.

Delivery

09

GitLab CI, fail closed

Stages, runners, a registry, rollback, and pipelines that fail on secret and dependency scans.

EvidenceA planted secret blocks the merge. A bad release is rolled back.

10

Kubernetes

Pods, Deployments, Services, probes, and a local cluster. The first deploy runs as non-root.

EvidenceA multi-replica app, then a broken rollout read from events and logs.

11

Cluster operations

Ingress, storage, Helm, network policy, the autoscaler, security contexts, and RBAC.

EvidenceA Helm release running as a restricted service account.

12

The secure pipeline

SAST, dependencies, secrets, images, infrastructure, a DAST pass in test, and a software bill of materials. Findings are fixed or explicitly accepted.

EvidenceA planted finding stops the pipeline until the fix is in the history.

Operations

13

Prometheus, Grafana, Alertmanager

Scrape, PromQL, dashboards, and Alertmanager routing, grouping, and silences. One route is repeated login failure.

EvidenceTested routes for CPU, disk, downtime, and authentication failure.

14

Logging with ELK

Structured logs, Elasticsearch, Kibana, Filebeat or Logstash, retention, and redaction.

EvidenceAn investigation that names an error or an authentication failure.

15

GitOps with Argo CD

Desired state, sync, drift, promotion, and rollback. A manifest still comes from a pipeline that passed.

EvidenceDeployed from Git, drift corrected, then promoted and rolled back.

16

Capstone

Architecture, security, cost, an incident, and a handover. The review asks what the last scan refused to ship.

EvidenceThe platform, the runbook, and a live demonstration.

Week 16

The capstone

A task manager, an inventory service, a student portal, or an API. The standard is a system a stranger could operate.

01

Source. GitLab, with a branching model and merge requests.

02

Cloud. Terraform on AWS. Least privilege, tags, and a cost note.

03

Pipeline. GitLab CI tests, builds, scans, and publishes. A failed gate blocks the merge.

04

Cluster. Kubernetes with probes, limits, and RBAC. EKS only if the budget allows.

05

Release. Argo CD from Git. Promotion and rollback, shown.

06

Signals. Prometheus, Grafana, Alertmanager, and an investigation in ELK.

07

Gates. A planted finding blocked the pipeline. The fix is in the history. An SBOM is kept.

08

Record. Diagram, README, findings, runbook, and a live demonstration.

GitLab CI is the pipeline. Secret detection may be GitLab's or gitleaks. Images and Terraform may be scanned with Trivy or Checkov. SAST may be GitLab or Semgrep. The DAST exercise may use OWASP ZAP against the test environment. The point is the gate, not the logo.

The studio

How the lab is run

01
16 GB of memory once local Kubernetes begins. 8 GB is the floor.
02
Linux by virtual machine, WSL2, or a dedicated machine. GitLab and a registry are provided.
03
AWS accounts use MFA, a budget alert, least privilege, and teardown after every lab.
04
Kubernetes runs on kind or Minikube. EKS is reserved for when the budget allows.
05
Credentials never go into Git. The scanner catches the mistake. It does not excuse it.
