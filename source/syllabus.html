DevSecOps AcademyThe syllabus

Build nothing you cannot scan, release, and explain.

Graduates leave with a platform they can defend: infrastructure in code, a pipeline that stops when a scan fails, a Kubernetes release, and the signals that say whether it is healthy.

01

Practice

Seventy percent of the hours are labs, projects, and review. Three instructor-led sessions a week, and two to four hours between them.

02

Judgement

A working demo is not enough. Cost, failure, access, and the scan result are graded with the feature.

03

Proof

The capstone is a system another engineer could operate from the runbook, without a private explanation.

Who it is for

Beginners with sound computer skills, students, developers, and engineers moving into cloud and security-minded delivery. Basic Linux is useful. A short orientation is provided before Week 1.

A graduate can

02
Ship through Git and GitLab, and stop a secret before it merges.

Build a Docker image, scan it, and refuse to publish what the scan condemns.
04
Provision AWS with least privilege and say what it costs.
05
Write that infrastructure in Terraform and scan the plan before apply.
06
Configure the host with Ansible so a second run changes nothing.
07
Release to Kubernetes through GitLab CI and Argo CD, and roll a bad release back.
08
Page on a real signal, and investigate it in the logs.

03Sixteen weeks
DevSecOps AcademyMonth 01

Weeks 01 - 04

Foundations

The machine, then the account
01

Linux and the shell

Distributions, the filesystem, and navigation. Users, groups, permissions, ownership, and sudo treated as controls. Packages, processes, systemd, logs, and disk. Bash variables, pipes, grep, find, awk, sed, and scripts.

EvidenceA documented server baseline with least privilege, and a Bash report of disk, memory, uptime, and service health.

02

Networking, SSH, and troubleshooting

IP addressing, CIDR, DNS, DHCP, routing, and ports. HTTP, TLS, firewalls, and security groups. SSH keys, scp, rsync, curl, dig, and ss. Patching, and a host baseline of listening ports and failed logins.

EvidenceTwo Linux hosts with key-based SSH, and a runbook for a deliberately broken web service.

03

Git, GitLab, and secret scanning

Commits, branches, merges, tags, reset, revert, and conflicts. Issues, merge requests, review, and protected branches. READMEs, versioning, and release notes. Secret scanning on the repository, so a credential cannot merge.

EvidenceA team project with reviews and tagged releases, plus a planted secret that the scanner blocks.

04

AWS fundamentals

Regions, Availability Zones, and shared responsibility. IAM users, roles, policies, MFA, least privilege, and the CLI. EC2, EBS, S3, VPC, subnets, route tables, and security groups. Budgets, tagging, CloudTrail, and an access review.

EvidenceA web server on EC2 inside a restricted VPC, and a static site on S3, with the security and cost decisions written down.

04Foundations
DevSecOps AcademyMonth 02

Weeks 05 - 08

Provision

Image, cloud, code
05

Docker, and the image scan

Containers and virtual machines. Images, registries, layers, and Dockerfiles, including multi-stage builds. Ports, volumes, networks, health checks, and Compose. Resource limits. An image scan, with critical findings treated as defects.

EvidenceAn application and database in Compose, an optimized image, and a scan report with the serious findings fixed.

06

AWS architecture

Launch templates, Auto Scaling, and an Application Load Balancer. S3 lifecycle, IAM roles for services, Lambda, and events. CloudWatch metrics, logs, and alarms. Public and private subnets, NAT, Secrets Manager, and Systems Manager.

EvidenceA highly available sample behind a load balancer, and a Lambda with logs and a CloudWatch alarm.

07

Terraform, scanned before apply

Providers, resources, variables, outputs, and data sources. Init, validate, plan, apply, destroy, and state. Modules, remote state, locking, and separate environments. An infrastructure scan of the plan. Drift, tagging, secrets, and cost.

EvidenceA VPC, subnets, security groups, EC2, and S3 in modules. The scan runs before apply. The stack is then destroyed.

08

Ansible

Inventory, modules, facts, and playbooks. Variables, handlers, templates, roles, and Vault. Idempotence, SSH, and privilege escalation. A short hardening pass: updates, SSH, and the firewall. Terraform builds the machine. Ansible configures it.

EvidenceA role that installs Nginx and sets a firewall rule, then a second run that changes nothing.

05Provision
DevSecOps AcademyMonth 03

Weeks 09 - 12

Delivery

Pipeline, cluster, gates
09

GitLab CI, fail closed

Stages, jobs, runners, artifacts, caches, and variables. Rules, environments, and approvals. Build, test, and publish an image. Rolling, blue/green, and canary as ideas, plus a real rollback. Secret and dependency scans fail the pipeline.

EvidenceA pipeline that tests and pushes on merge, a planted secret that blocks it, and a demonstrated rollback.

10

Kubernetes

Control plane, nodes, API server, scheduler, and etcd. Pods, Deployments, Services, namespaces, and labels. ConfigMaps, Secrets, probes, requests, and limits. kubectl on kind or Minikube. The first deploy runs as a non-root user.

EvidenceA multi-replica application with a Service and probes, then a broken rollout diagnosed from events, logs, and history.

11

Cluster operations

Ingress and network policy. Persistent volumes and stateful workloads. The Horizontal Pod Autoscaler. Helm values, upgrade, and rollback. Security contexts, Pod Security, and RBAC for a restricted account.

EvidenceA Helm chart with configurable values, persistent storage, and a service account that can do only its job.

12

The secure pipeline

SAST, dependency scanning, secret scanning, image scanning, and an infrastructure scan, all in GitLab CI. A DAST pass against the test environment. A software bill of materials. Minimal images, pinned versions, no embedded secrets. Findings are fixed or explicitly accepted.

EvidenceA planted finding stops the pipeline. The fix is committed. The image runs as non-root. The SBOM is an artifact of the build.

DevSecOps AcademyMonth 04

Weeks 13 - 16

Operations

Signal, then release
13

Prometheus, Grafana, and Alertmanager

Metrics, logs, and traces. Golden signals, and alerts that stay quiet. Prometheus scrape, exporters, labels, PromQL, and alert rules. Alertmanager grouping, routing, inhibition, silences, and receivers. Grafana for the picture. Alertmanager for the page. One route is a security signal: repeated authentication failure.

EvidenceA dashboard, and tested routes for CPU, disk, downtime, and login failure, with a short response note.

14

Logging with ELK

Structured logs, levels, and correlation IDs. Elasticsearch indexes. Kibana Discover and dashboards. Ingestion with Elastic Agent, Filebeat, or Logstash. Retention, access, and redaction of secrets that must not sit in the index.

EvidenceApplication and system logs in Elasticsearch, a Kibana dashboard, and a written investigation of an error or an authentication failure.

15

GitOps with Argo CD

Desired state, reconciliation, and drift. Applications, sync policy, and health. Source kept apart from deployment manifests. Promotion, rollback, and a secrets approach. GitOps does not bypass the scan gates. A manifest still comes from a pipeline that passed.

EvidenceA release deployed from Git, a drift correction, a promotion, and a rollback.

16

Capstone and demonstration

Architecture, reliability, security, cost, and the writing, judged together. An incident exercise. A handover. The review asks how a bad release is undone, which signal would page someone, and what the last scan refused to ship.

EvidenceThe platform on the next page, demonstrated live.

The capstone

A task manager, an inventory service, a student portal, or an API. Individual or a small team. The diagram and the running system describe the same architecture.

01

GitLab, with a branching model and merge requests.
02
Cloud
Terraform on AWS. Least privilege, tags, and a cost note. Ansible where a host must be configured.
03

GitLab CI that tests, builds, scans, and publishes. A failed gate blocks the merge.
04

Kubernetes with probes, requests and limits, and RBAC. EKS where the budget allows. kind or Minikube otherwise.
05
Release
Argo CD from Git, with promotion and rollback shown.
06
Signals
Prometheus, Grafana, and Alertmanager routes that have fired. Logs in ELK, used for one investigation.
07
Gates
Secrets, dependencies, SAST, the image, and the Terraform plan. A planted finding, and the fix in history. An SBOM from the build.
08
Record
Architecture diagram, README, findings, runbook, and a live demonstration.

The tools, named once

GitLab CI is the pipeline. Secret detection may be GitLab's or gitleaks. Images and Terraform may be scanned with Trivy or Checkov. SAST may be GitLab or Semgrep. The DAST exercise may use OWASP ZAP against the test environment. The point is the gate, not the logo.

What is graded

A claim without a repository, a diagram, or a demonstration does not count.

Weekly labs25%The work of the week, including the troubleshooting.
Projects and review20%Deliverables, the writing, and the hygiene of the Git history.
Mid-programme practical15%Docker, AWS, Terraform, Ansible, and a pipeline that scans.
Security and signals15%Gates that fired, a fix, dashboards, logs, and alerts.

The shape of a week

Session 1

The idea

The concept, a worked demonstration, and the decision the lab will force.

Session 2

The lab

Guided practice, with checkpoints where the system is expected to break.

Session 3

The review

Project work, someone else's change, a short check, and a presentation.

Between

The hours

Two to four hours for practice, writing, and the portfolio.

To finish

Complete at least 80 percent of the labs. Submit the projects and pass the mid-programme practical. Demonstrate the capstone. In the review, explain the security decisions, the recovery from a bad release, the signal that would page someone, and the cost.

How the lab is run

Accounts, machines, and secrets are handled the way a serious team handles them. Cost control is part of the work.

Practice

01
16 GB of memory is the recommendation once local Kubernetes begins. 8 GB is the floor.
02
Linux by virtual machine, WSL2, or a dedicated machine. GitLab and a container registry are provided.
03
AWS learner accounts use MFA, a budget alert, least privilege, and teardown at the end of every lab.
04
Kubernetes exercises run on kind or Minikube. EKS is reserved for when the budget allows.
05
Credentials never go into Git. A scanner is there to catch the mistake, not to excuse it.
06
Every project leaves a repository, a README, a diagram where the system has one, and a short demonstration.
